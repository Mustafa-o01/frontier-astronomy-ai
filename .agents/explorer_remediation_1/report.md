## 3. Physical & Mathematical Foundations

### 3.1 Exoplanet Transmission Spectrophotometry Forward Model
An exoplanet of radius $R_p$ orbiting a star of radius $R_*$ produces an apparent transit depth $D(\lambda)$:
$$D(\lambda) = \left(\frac{R_p(\lambda)}{R_*}\right)^2 \approx \left(\frac{R_p(P_0)}{R_*}\right)^2 + \frac{2 R_p(P_0) H}{R_*^2} \ln\left(\frac{P_0}{P(\tau=1)}\right)$$
where $H$ is the atmospheric scale height:
$$H = \frac{k_B T_{\mathrm{eq}}}{\mu g}$$
- $T_{\mathrm{eq}}$: Atmospheric equilibrium temperature ($400 - 2500\,\mathrm{K}$)
- $\mu$: Mean molecular weight ($\approx 2.3 m_u$ for $\mathrm{H}_2/\mathrm{He}$)
- $g = G M_p / R_p^2$: Planetary surface gravity
- The effective transit depth modulation $\Delta D(\lambda)$ scales with the total atmospheric optical depth $\tau(\lambda)$ across absorption lines of $\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}$, $\mathrm{CO}_2$ (4.3 $\mu\mathrm{m}$ peak), $\mathrm{CH}_4$ (3.3 $\mu\mathrm{m}$), $\mathrm{CO}$ (4.7 $\mu\mathrm{m}$), Rayleigh haze ($\propto \lambda^{-\gamma}$), and is truncated at high cloud top pressure $P_c$ (grey cloud floor).

### 3.2 Amortized Neural Posterior Estimation (NPE) with Normalizing Flows
In Bayesian retrieval, we seek the posterior parameter distribution $p(\theta | x)$ over the 7 atmospheric parameters:
$$\theta = \left[\log_{10} X_{\mathrm{H2O}}, \log_{10} X_{\mathrm{CO2}}, \log_{10} X_{\mathrm{CH4}}, \log_{10} X_{\mathrm{CO}}, T_{\mathrm{eq}}, \log_{10} P_c, \mathrm{haze\_slope}\right]$$
conditioned on the observed transmission spectrum $x \in \mathbb{R}^M$.

Normalizing Flows transform a simple base distribution $u \sim p_u(u) = \mathcal{N}(0, I_D)$ into the complex posterior $q_\phi(\theta | x)$ via an invertible, differentiable mapping $g_x(u)$ with tractable Jacobian determinant:
$$\ln q_\phi(\theta | x) = \ln p_u(g_x^{-1}(\theta)) - \ln \left| \det J_{g_x}(g_x^{-1}(\theta)) \right|$$

In **Conditional RealNVP**:
Each affine coupling layer partitions the parameters $\theta = [\theta_A, \theta_B]$:
$$\theta_A' = \theta_A$$
$$\theta_B' = \theta_B \odot \exp(s(\theta_A, E(x))) + t(\theta_A, E(x))$$
where $E(x)$ is a neural context encoder mapping spectral channels to a latent embedding, and $s, t$ are multi-layer perceptrons.

During training, the network is trained via negative log-likelihood minimization on pairs $(\theta_k, x_k)$ generated by the forward radiative transfer simulator:
$$\mathcal{L}(\phi) = - \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^K \ln q_\phi(\theta_k | x_k)$$

During inference:
1. Pass $x_{\mathrm{obs}}$ into context encoder $E(x_{\mathrm{obs}})$.
2. Draw $S$ standard normal samples $u \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_7)$.
3. Evaluate $\theta = g_{x_{\mathrm{obs}}}(u)$ in a single vectorized forward pass.
Runtime for $S = 2,000$ samples is **$3 - 6\,\mathrm{milliseconds}$**, delivering orders of magnitude faster execution than MCMC ($10^4$ seconds).

## 4. Authentic Remediation Architecture

### 4.1 Invariant Spectral Feature Representation
To allow the neural network to handle any exoplanet (different star/planet radii, different base transit depths) and any instrument (NIRSpec PRISM covering $0.6 - 5.3\,\mu\mathrm{m}$, NIRISS SOSS covering $0.6 - 2.8\,\mu\mathrm{m}$):
1. **Baseline Invariance**: Compute baseline continuum transit depth $D_{\mathrm{base}} = \mathrm{median}(D_{\mathrm{obs}})$.
2. **Relative Excess**: Compute relative spectral modulation $\Delta D_{\mathrm{obs}}(\lambda) = D_{\mathrm{obs}}(\lambda) - D_{\mathrm{base}}$.
3. **Standard Channel Grid**: Interpolate $\Delta D_{\mathrm{obs}}$ onto standard 100 channels $\lambda_{\mathrm{std}} \in [0.6, 5.3]\,\mu\mathrm{m}$.
   - For wavelengths within the instrument coverage $[\min(\lambda_{\mathrm{obs}}), \max(\lambda_{\mathrm{obs}})]$, use interpolated $\Delta D$.
   - For wavelengths outside the instrument coverage (e.g. $\lambda > 2.8\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ for NIRISS), set excess to $0.0$ (representing no observed excess over baseline).
4. **Standard Scaling**: Multiply by $10^3$ so spectral channel values are $\mathcal{O}(1)$ ($\sim 0.1 - 2.0$), optimizing neural network gradient propagation.
