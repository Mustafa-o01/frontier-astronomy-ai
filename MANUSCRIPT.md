## Abstract

We present a systematic re-evaluation of four candidate astrophysical signals identified during an automated algorithmic search across archival *Kepler* space telescope light curves. The initial automated classification pipeline flagged four objects of interest: (1) **KIC 9944201** (KOI K07259.01) as a catastrophic disintegrating rocky exoplanet displaying an asymmetric cometary dust tail with an anomalous Bayesian Information Criterion difference ($\Delta\mathrm{BIC} \approx +73,043$); (2) **KIC 8494263** (KOI K01255.01) as an exomoon companion candidate exhibiting a claimed Transit Timing Variation (TTV) signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of 50.1 and an exomoon posterior probability of $99.9\%$; (3) **KIC 10153011** (KOI K01773.01) as an additional exomoon candidate with claimed TTV $\mathrm{SNR} = 40.3$ ($P = 99.9\%$); and (4) **KIC 8308347** (KOI K03761.01) as an $L_4/L_5$ co-orbital Trojan planetary companion. 

Here, we conduct an exhaustive astrophysical, photodynamical, and statistical forensic audit of these claims. We demonstrate that:
1. The extraordinary $\Delta\mathrm{BIC} \approx +73,043$ for **KIC 9944201** is a mathematical artifact resulting from evaluating unbinned cadences ($N = 8,431$) under independent white-noise likelihood assumptions in the presence of unmodeled stellar variability and eclipsing binary geometry. Phase-curve inspection reveals a distinct secondary eclipse at phase $\phi \approx 0.5$ with a depth of $\approx 5,300\ \mathrm{ppm}$ alongside a primary minimum of $\approx 22,500\ \mathrm{ppm}$ on an ultra-short orbital period of $P \approx 0.7215\ \mathrm{d}$. Combined with stellar host parameters ($R_* \approx 0.625\ R_\odot$, $T_\mathrm{eff} \approx 4,893\ \mathrm{K}$) that imply a transit depth corresponding to a Jupiter-sized body ($R_p \approx 11.0\ R_\oplus$) rather than an evaporating sub-Earth rocky core, KIC 9944201 is decisively consistent with an eclipsing binary (EB) or background eclipsing binary (BEB), completely falsifying the disintegrating rocky crust hypothesis.
2. The high TTV SNRs ($>40$) and extreme exomoon posteriors ($99.9\%$) for **KIC 8494263** and **KIC 10153011** were generated by an unmasked polynomial baseline detrending filter that eroded the in-transit profiles across sparse datasets spanning only $N = 3$ observed transit events. On raw, uncontaminated photometry, KIC 8494263 displays timing variations of only $\sim 10$ seconds ($\mathrm{SNR} \approx 0.19$), while KIC 10153011 displays $\mathrm{SNR} \approx 2.20$, well below the statistical detection threshold ($\mathrm{SNR} \ge 3.0$). Neither system exhibits the pathognomonic $\pi/2$ phase shift between TTV and Transit Duration Variations (TDV). Furthermore, the claimed $99.9\%$ probability was derived from an uncalibrated empirical heuristic mapping function rather than genuine marginal likelihood integration.
3. **KIC 8308347** contains only two transit epochs in the scanned baseline, with zero successfully resolved timing residuals. The claimed Trojan dip at phase $\pm 60^\circ$ is attributable to stellar rotational spot modulation and red noise.

We conclude that none of the four objects can be classified as bona fide detections of disintegrating planets, exomoons, or Trojans. All four are re-classified as false positives or unverified candidate artifacts. We outline strict verification protocols and Bayesian requirements necessary to prevent automated pipelines from mistaking data-reduction artifacts for revolutionary astrophysical discoveries.

## 1. Introduction

The search for second-order astrophysical phenomena in high-precision space-based transit photometry—such as catastrophically evaporating exoplanets (Rappaport et al. 2012, 2014; Sanchis-Ojeda et al. 2015), natural satellites orbiting extrasolar gas giants (exomoons; Sartoretti & Schneider 1999; Kipping 2009a, 2012; Teachey & Kipping 2018; Kipping et al. 2022), and co-orbital Trojan companions (Ford & Holman 2007; Jontof-Hutter et al. 2015)—represents one of the most exciting frontiers in observational exoplanetary science. 

These exotic phenomena carry profound physical implications:
- Disintegrating rocky planets offer unique observational laboratories to probe the core composition and mineralogy of terrestrial bodies via escaping dust and gas (Perez-Becker & Chiang 2013; van Lieshout et al. 2014).
- Exomoons provide constraints on planetary formation mechanisms, satellite migration, and circumplanetary disk dynamics, while expanding the scope of potentially habitable environments (Heller & Barnes 2013).
- Co-orbital Trojan planets yield insights into early planetary migration, protoplanetary disk gas damping, and orbital resonance capture (Laughlin & Chambers 2002; Beaugé et al. 2007).

However, detecting these signals in archival data from missions such as *Kepler* (Borucki et al. 2010), *K2* (Howell et al. 2014), and the *Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite* (TESS; Ricker et al. 2015) is fraught with extreme observational challenges. The photometric signatures sought are typically subtle ($\sim 10^{-4}$ to $10^{-5}$ in relative flux) or temporally variable, lying at the edge of instrumental and astrophysical noise floors. 

In recent years, the deployment of automated discovery suites, machine-learning classifiers, and algorithmic grid searches has accelerated candidate generation. Yet, algorithmic autonomy introduces severe vulnerabilities. When complex multi-parameter models are evaluated on unbinned data without rigorous accounting for correlated (red) stellar noise, or when non-linear detrending filters distort underlying transit shapes, automated statistical metrics can report astronomical statistical significances for purely instrumental or data-reduction artifacts.

In this work, we conduct a forensic re-evaluation of four prominent candidate detections generated by an automated discovery pipeline applied to archival *Kepler* data:
1. **KIC 9944201** (KOI K07259.01): Flagged as a disintegrating rocky body with a trailing dust tail ($\Delta\mathrm{BIC} \approx +73,043$).
2. **KIC 8494263** (KOI K01255.01): Flagged as an exomoon host with $\mathrm{TTV\ SNR} = 50.1$ and claimed posterior $P(\mathrm{moon}) = 99.9\%$.
3. **KIC 10153011** (KOI K01773.01): Flagged as an exomoon host with $\mathrm{TTV\ SNR} = 40.3$ and claimed posterior $P(\mathrm{moon}) = 99.9\%$.
4. **KIC 8308347** (KOI K03761.01): Flagged as hosting an $L_4/L_5$ co-orbital Trojan companion.

We establish the mathematical, physical, and observational origins of these spurious classifications, perform genuine astrophysical tests (including secondary eclipse searches, out-of-transit variability checks, and uncorrupted timing analyses), and outline the mandatory validation standards required for future automated exoplanet surveys.

## 3. Data Reduction & Detrending Vulnerabilities

### 3.1 Photometric Ingestion & Quality Flags
Photometric time-series were ingested from Pre-search Data Conditioning Simple Aperture Photometry (PDCSAP) FITS data tables. Nominal quality filtering discards cadences flagged with severe spacecraft anomalies, including momentum desaturations, safe modes, Earth-pointing maneuvers, and coarse pointing flags (Kepler `SAP_QUALITY > 0`).

### 3.2 Detrending & The Critical Danger of Unmasked Filtering
To isolate transit events and timing perturbations from low-frequency stellar rotational variability and instrumental thermal drifts, time-series are typically normalized using running medians, Gaussian Process regression, or Savitzky-Golay (SG) polynomial filters.

The automated pipeline applied an iterative Savitzky-Golay filter with a nominal window length of $W = 1.0\ \mathrm{day}$ and polynomial order $p = 2$. However, an inspection of the preprocessing pipeline revealed a critical flaw: when the orbital parameters were passed without an explicit, pre-computed physical transit duration ($T_{14}$), the routine defaulted to an unmasked filter:
$$\hat{f}(t) = \sum_{j=-M}^{M} c_j f(t + j \Delta t)$$
Because the filter window ($1.0\ \mathrm{day}$) was comparable to or only a few times larger than the transit duration (for instance, $T_{14} \approx 4\text{--}8\ \mathrm{hours}$ for long-period gas giants), the filter treated the in-transit photometric dip as stellar variability. Consequently:
1. The continuum fit $\hat{f}(t)$ bowed downward into the transit basin.
2. The normalized flux $f_\mathrm{norm}(t) = f(t) / \hat{f}(t)$ suffered asymmetric erosion at the ingress and egress shoulders.
3. The fitted transit midpoint was artificially shifted by tens of minutes depending on the local cadence distribution and polynomial curvature.

As demonstrated in Section 5, this data-reduction distortion was the sole cause of the anomalous TTV and exomoon signatures detected in KIC 8494263 and KIC 10153011.

## 4. Detection Methodology & Mathematical Formulation

### 4.1 Cometary Dust Tail Forward Model
For a catastrophic disintegrating rocky exoplanet, dust grains ejected from the sublimating substellar surface are driven radially outward by stellar radiation pressure, forming a trailing cometary tail (Rappaport et al. 2012, 2014; Brogi et al. 2012; van Lieshout & Rappaport 2018).

The extinction optical depth $\tau(\phi)$ as a function of orbital phase $\phi$ is parameterized by a sharp, Gaussian-like ingress representing the planetary body and leading cloud, followed by an asymmetric exponential decay:
$$\tau(\phi) = \begin{cases} 
\tau_\mathrm{max} \exp\left[ -\frac{(\phi - \phi_0)^2}{2 \sigma_\mathrm{ing}^2} \right], & \phi < \phi_0 \\ 
\tau_\mathrm{max} \exp\left[ -\left( \frac{\phi - \phi_0}{\lambda_\mathrm{tail}} \right)^\alpha \right], & \phi \ge \phi_0 
\end{cases}$$
where:
- $\tau_\mathrm{max}$ is the peak optical depth at mid-transit,
- $\sigma_\mathrm{ing}$ is the ingress width,
- $\lambda_\mathrm{tail}$ is the characteristic tail decay scale,
- $\alpha$ is the power-law tail decay exponent (typically $\alpha \in [0.5, 2.0]$), and
- $\phi_0$ is the reference orbital phase offset.

Forward scattering by micron-sized dust grains (Mie scattering) can produce a pre-transit photometric brightening ("scattering bump") before ingress:
$$F_\mathrm{scat}(\phi) = f_\mathrm{scat} \exp\left[ -\frac{(\phi - \phi_\mathrm{scat})^2}{2 \sigma_\mathrm{scat}^2} \right]$$
The net normalized flux model is:
$$F_\mathrm{tail}(\phi) = 1 - \tau(\phi) + F_\mathrm{scat}(\phi)$$

### 4.2 Model Selection Statistics and the False Security of $\Delta\mathrm{BIC}$
To evaluate whether an asymmetric cometary dust tail model is justified over a standard symmetric planetary transit, the pipeline compares $F_\mathrm{tail}(\phi)$ ($k_\mathrm{tail} = 8$ free parameters) against a symmetric trapezoidal or limb-darkened model $F_\mathrm{sym}(\phi)$ ($k_\mathrm{sym} = 5$ free parameters).

The Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) is defined as:
$$\mathrm{BIC} = k \ln(N) - 2 \ln \mathcal{L}_\mathrm{max}$$
Assuming independent and identically distributed Gaussian photometric uncertainties $\sigma_i$, the log-likelihood reduces to:
$$-2 \ln \mathcal{L}_\mathrm{max} = \chi^2 = \sum_{i=1}^N \left( \frac{f_i - F(\phi_i)}{\sigma_i} \right)^2$$
yielding:
$$\Delta\mathrm{BIC} = \mathrm{BIC}_\mathrm{sym} - \mathrm{BIC}_\mathrm{tail} = (\chi^2_\mathrm{sym} - \chi^2_\mathrm{tail}) + (k_\mathrm{sym} - k_\mathrm{tail}) \ln(N)$$

> [!WARNING]
> **Fundamental Breakdown of $\Delta\mathrm{BIC}$ in Correlated Noise:**
> When $N$ is large (e.g., $N = 8,431$ cadences) and the true stellar baseline contains correlated (red) noise, stellar spots, or unmodeled binary components, the formal measurement uncertainties $\sigma_i$ are substantially smaller than the model residuals ($\chi^2 / \nu \gg 1$). Under these conditions, an 8-parameter flexible function will absorb arbitrary residual stellar variance, producing an enormous $\Delta\chi^2 \sim 10^4\text{--}10^5$. 
> 
> A value of $\Delta\mathrm{BIC} \approx +73,043$ does **not** signify a $e^{36,500}:1$ posterior probability in favor of a cometary tail. Rather, it indicates severe model misspecification: neither model describes the underlying data, and the symmetric model was trapped in a suboptimal local minimum.

### 4.3 Transit Timing Variation (TTV) Extraction
For each observed epoch $E$, the mid-transit time $t_\mathrm{mid}(E)$ is measured by cross-correlating a high-SNR transit template with the in-transit photometric cadences and refining the minimum via a 3-point parabolic interpolation:
$$\chi^2(\tau) = \sum_{i \in \mathrm{epoch}} \frac{\left(f_i - F_\mathrm{template}(t_i - \tau)\right)^2}{\sigma_i^2}$$
The formal timing uncertainty is derived from the local curvature of the likelihood surface:
$$\sigma_\tau = \left( \frac{1}{2} \frac{\partial^2 \chi^2}{\partial \tau^2} \right)^{-1/2}$$
A linear ephemeris is fitted across all observed epochs via weighted least-squares:
$$t_\mathrm{calc}(E) = t_0 + E \cdot P$$
yielding the Observed minus Calculated ($O - C$) timing residuals:
$$\mathrm{TTV}(E) = t_\mathrm{mid}(E) - t_\mathrm{calc}(E)$$

### 4.4 The Orthogonal $\pi/2$ Phase Invariant for Exomoon Validation
In a genuine planet-satellite system, the planet orbits the mutual planet-moon barycenter with velocity $v_\mathrm{p,bary}$, while the barycenter orbits the host star with Keplerian velocity $v_B$ (Sartoretti & Schneider 1999; Kipping 2009a, 2012). 

This configuration produces two coupled, strictly periodic observational effects:
1. **Transit Timing Variations (TTV):** Caused by the spatial displacement of the planet along the orbital path:
   $$\delta t \approx \frac{a_{sp}}{v_B} \frac{M_s}{M_p + M_s} \sin(\varpi_s)$$
2. **Transit Duration Variations (TDV):** Caused by the variation in the planet's velocity relative to the star during transit:
   $$\delta T \approx T_0 \frac{v_\mathrm{p,bary}}{v_B} \cos(\varpi_s)$$

Because $\sin(\varpi_s)$ and $\cos(\varpi_s)$ are orthogonal harmonic functions, the TTV and TDV signals must exhibit a **strict $\pi/2$ ($90^\circ$) phase offset**:
$$\Delta \phi(\mathrm{TTV}, \mathrm{TDV}) = 90^\circ \pm 15^\circ$$
In contrast:
- Planet-planet Mean Motion Resonances (MMRs) produce TTVs and TDVs that are collinear ($0^\circ$ or $180^\circ$).
- Stellar spot crossings and detrending distortions produce uncorrelated, stochastic phase differences.

### 4.5 The Uncalibrated Heuristic Fallacy: $P(\mathrm{moon}) = 99.9\%$
In rigorous Bayesian inference, the posterior probability of an exomoon hypothesis $\mathcal{M}_\mathrm{moon}$ relative to an isolated planet hypothesis $\mathcal{M}_\mathrm{null}$ requires calculating the Bayes factor $B_{10}$:
$$B_{10} = \frac{\mathcal{Z}_\mathrm{moon}}{\mathcal{Z}_\mathrm{null}} = \frac{\int \mathcal{L}(\theta_\mathrm{moon}) \pi(\theta_\mathrm{moon}) d\theta_\mathrm{moon}}{\int \mathcal{L}(\theta_\mathrm{null}) \pi(\theta_\mathrm{null}) d\theta_\mathrm{null}}$$
where $\mathcal{Z}$ is the Bayesian evidence integrated over the physical 3-body photodynamical parameter space via nested sampling (e.g., MultiNest or Dynesty).

In the scanned pipeline, however, $P(\mathrm{moon})$ was computed using an ad-hoc empirical logit function:
$$\ln B_\mathrm{heuristic} = 0.8 \cdot (\mathrm{SNR}_\mathrm{TTV} - 3.0) - \frac{(\Delta\phi - 90^\circ)^2}{2(15^\circ)^2}$$
$$P_\mathrm{heuristic} = \frac{1}{1 + \exp(-\ln B_\mathrm{heuristic})}$$

When $\mathrm{SNR}_\mathrm{TTV} = 50.1$, the first term evaluates to $+37.7$. Even with a heavy penalty for phase misalignment ($\Delta\phi = 0^\circ$), the net $\ln B$ remains $> +14$, yielding:
$$P = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-14.7}} = 0.999999 \rightarrow 99.9\%$$
This proves that the reported $99.9\%$ confidence was a direct mathematical artifact of an uncalibrated sigmoid function applied to an inflated SNR, **not** genuine Bayesian evidence.

## 5. Forensic Audit & Re-Evaluation of Candidate Signals

### 5.1 KIC 9944201 (KOI K07259.01) — Disintegrating Planet vs. Eclipsing Binary
- **Catalog Parameters:** $P = 0.7215229\ \mathrm{d}$, $t_0 = 131.7091\ \mathrm{BKJD}$, Transit Depth $\delta = 22,528\ \mathrm{ppm}$ ($2.25\%$). Host: $R_* = 0.625\ R_\odot$, $T_\mathrm{eff} = 4,893\ \mathrm{K}$ (K-dwarf).
- **Claim:** Disintegrating rocky crust with trailing cometary dust tail ($\Delta\mathrm{BIC} \approx +73,043$, Asymmetry $\alpha = 0.534$).

#### Astrophysical Impossibility of Rocky Disintegration
All known confirmed or validated disintegrating rocky exoplanets—such as KIC 12557548 b (Rappaport et al. 2012), KOI-2700 b (Rappaport et al. 2014), and K2-22 b (Sanchis-Ojeda et al. 2015)—exhibit transit depths between $0.1\%$ and $1.3\%$, originating from bodies with catastrophic mass loss rates ($\dot{M} \sim 0.1\text{--}1\ M_\oplus / \mathrm{Gyr}$) powered by thermal escape from ultra-short-period rocky cores with initial radii $R_p \lesssim 1\text{--}2\ R_\oplus$ (Perez-Becker & Chiang 2013).

For KIC 9944201, a transit depth of $2.25\%$ around an $R_* = 0.625\ R_\odot$ star requires an occulting area:
$$A_\mathrm{eff} = \delta \cdot \pi R_*^2 \approx 0.0225 \cdot \pi (0.625 \cdot 6.96 \times 10^8\ \mathrm{m})^2 \approx 1.34 \times 10^{16}\ \mathrm{m}^2$$
corresponding to an equivalent physical radius:
$$R_\mathrm{eff} = \sqrt{\delta} \cdot R_* \approx 0.15 \cdot 0.625\ R_\odot \approx 0.0938\ R_\odot \approx 1.02\ R_\mathrm{Jup} \approx 11.04\ R_\oplus$$
A solid disintegrating rocky body cannot sustain an occulting cloud of $1.0\ R_\mathrm{Jup}$ at $T_\mathrm{eq} = 1,475\ \mathrm{K}$ without complete catastrophic vaporization within a few thousand years.

#### Discovery of the Secondary Eclipse
We phase-folded the full calibrated *Kepler* light curve across 8,431 cadences ($218\ \mathrm{days}$, $\sim 302$ orbital cycles) and constructed an inverse-variance binned phase curve across 50 orbital phase bins. The binned photometry reveals:
1. A primary transit/eclipse minimum at phase $\phi = 0.00$ with flux $F = 0.9800$ (depth $= 20,000\ \mathrm{ppm}$).
2. A distinct, unambiguous **secondary eclipse at phase $\phi = \pm 0.50$** with flux $F = 0.9947$ (depth $= 5,300\ \mathrm{ppm} = 0.53\%$).
3. Continuous out-of-transit ellipsoidal variation and reflection modulation with a peak-to-peak amplitude of $4,151\ \mathrm{ppm}$ ($0.42\%$).

The presence of a $0.53\%$ secondary eclipse exactly at phase $0.5$ in a circularized $P = 0.72\ \mathrm{day}$ orbit is the definitive, pathognomonic signature of a **stellar eclipsing binary (EB)** or a blended background eclipsing binary (BEB). 

#### Deconstructing the $\Delta\mathrm{BIC} \approx 73,043$
When the automated pipeline fitted the symmetric model, the least-squares optimizer became trapped in a local minimum ($\chi^2_\mathrm{sym} = 259,970$ for $N = 8,431$, giving a reduced $\chi^2_\nu \approx 30.8$). The 8-parameter cometary model, possessing free parameters for forward-scattering brightening and exponential egress wings, absorbed portions of the out-of-transit ellipsoidal variability, reducing $\chi^2$ to $186,900$ ($\chi^2_\nu \approx 22.2$). 

The resulting difference:
$$\Delta\mathrm{BIC} = (259,970 - 186,900) - (8 - 5)\ln(8,431) = 73,070 - 27.1 = 73,042.9$$
This confirms that the enormous $\Delta\mathrm{BIC}$ was merely a mathematical reflection of two badly fitting models applied to an eclipsing binary.

*Disposition:* **DEFINITIVE FALSE POSITIVE (Eclipsing Binary / BEB)**.

### 5.2 KIC 8494263 (KOI K01255.01) — Exomoon Claim vs. Sparse Cadence Artifact
- **Catalog Parameters:** $P = 78.92574\ \mathrm{d}$, $t_0 = 429.3745\ \mathrm{BKJD}$, Transit Depth $\delta = 17,972\ \mathrm{ppm}$ ($1.80\%$). Host: $R_* = 0.882\ R_\odot$, $T_\mathrm{eff} = 5,750\ \mathrm{K}$.
- **Claim:** Exomoon satellite detection via orthogonal TTV/TDV ($\mathrm{TTV\ SNR} = 50.1$, claimed $P(\mathrm{moon}) = 99.9\%$).

#### The Reality of Three Observed Transits
In the 276.9-day baseline analyzed, the orbital period of $78.93\ \mathrm{days}$ dictates that at most three transits could occur. We extracted the exact cadence timestamps and identified precisely **three transit events**:
- Epoch 0: $t = 429.3609\ \mathrm{BKJD}$
- Epoch 1: $t = 508.2979\ \mathrm{BKJD}$
- Epoch 2: $t = 587.2353\ \mathrm{BKJD}$

It is statistically impossible to establish a periodic sinusoidal TTV curve—let alone infer a dynamical satellite orbit—from $N = 3$ data points.

#### Dissecting the 50.1 SNR Artifact
When the pipeline detrended the light curve without masking the transits (Section 3.2), the Savitzky-Golay polynomial severely distorted the ingress and egress profiles of these three transits. This distortion artificially shifted the measured midpoint times, creating spurious timing residuals of:
$$\mathrm{TTV}_\mathrm{distorted} = [-43.7\ \mathrm{min},\ +99.0\ \mathrm{min},\ -114.1\ \mathrm{min}]$$
With an estimated timing error of $\sim 1.0\ \mathrm{min}$, the pipeline calculated:
$$\mathrm{SNR} = \frac{\mathrm{std}(\mathrm{TTV})}{\langle \sigma \rangle} \sqrt{2} \approx \frac{88.7}{1.1} \times 1.414 \approx 50.1$$

When we re-extracted the mid-transit times on the **raw, uncorrupted photometry** using linear baseline normalization, the true timing residuals collapsed to:
$$\mathrm{TTV}_\mathrm{true} = [+0.071\ \mathrm{min},\ -0.161\ \mathrm{min},\ +0.186\ \mathrm{min}] \approx [+4.3\ \mathrm{s},\ -9.6\ \mathrm{s},\ +11.2\ \mathrm{s}]$$
with realistic photometric timing uncertainties of $\sigma_\tau \approx [0.89\ \mathrm{min},\ 0.95\ \mathrm{min},\ 1.44\ \mathrm{min}]$.
The genuine timing signal-to-noise ratio is:
$$\mathrm{SNR}_\mathrm{true} = 0.187$$
Furthermore, the measured TDV values were zero within uncertainties, and the phase difference was $\Delta\phi = 0.0^\circ$ rather than the required $90^\circ$.

*Disposition:* **DEFINITIVE FALSE POSITIVE (Detrending Induced Timing Artifact)**.

### 5.3 KIC 10153011 (KOI K01773.01) — Exomoon Claim vs. Robovetter False Positive
- **Catalog Parameters:** $P = 83.09708\ \mathrm{d}$, $t_0 = 162.9357\ \mathrm{BKJD}$, Depth $\delta = 15,860\ \mathrm{ppm}$ ($1.59\%$).
- **Kepler Robovetter Catalog Score:** $0.000$ (Flagged as non-planetary in official DR25).
- **Claim:** Exomoon satellite detection ($\mathrm{TTV\ SNR} = 40.3$, claimed $P(\mathrm{moon}) = 99.9\%$).

Like KIC 8494263, KIC 10153011 was analyzed over a baseline of only $272.4\ \mathrm{days}$, capturing exactly three transits ($E = 1, 2, 3$). 

Re-analysis on the raw, uncorrupted light curve reveals:
- Measured timing residuals: $\mathrm{TTV} = [+0.83\ \mathrm{min},\ -1.72\ \mathrm{min},\ +1.04\ \mathrm{min}]$
- True timing signal-to-noise ratio: $\mathrm{SNR} = 2.20$

An SNR of $2.20$ is statistically indistinguishable from Gaussian white noise fluctuations ($p > 0.05$). The inflated $\mathrm{SNR} = 40.3$ and subsequent $P(\mathrm{moon}) = 99.9\%$ were entirely produced by the unmasked SG detrending filter and the empirical logit function.

*Disposition:* **REJECTED CANDIDATE / FALSE POSITIVE**.

### 5.4 KIC 8308347 (KOI K03761.01) — Trojan Candidate vs. Stellar Noise
- **Catalog Parameters:** $P = 164.9504\ \mathrm{d}$, $t_0 = 280.9154\ \mathrm{BKJD}$, Depth $\delta = 24,493\ \mathrm{ppm}$ ($2.45\%$).
- **Kepler Robovetter Catalog Score:** $0.000$.
- **Claim:** Co-orbital Trojan companion at $L_4/L_5$ ($\pm 60^\circ$ phase offset).

Across the analyzed baseline of $276.5\ \mathrm{days}$, with an orbital period of $164.95\ \mathrm{days}$, only one or two transit events exist in the data. In fact, our programmatic extraction confirms that zero valid transit midpoint fits were obtained due to data gaps. 

The pipeline flagged a "Trojan companion" based on a localized flux depression near phase $\phi \approx +0.1667$ ($+60^\circ$). However, in a light curve with only 1.6 orbital cycles, low-frequency stellar rotational modulation from active starspots will inevitably introduce stochastic troughs across arbitrary phases. Without demonstrating phase coherence and repeatable transit morphology across $>10$ consecutive orbits, an isolated dip cannot be classified as a co-orbital companion.

*Disposition:* **REJECTED CANDIDATE (Stellar Red Noise Artifact)**.

## 7. Synthetic Injection-Recovery Experiments

To evaluate the baseline sensitivity and recovery completeness of the physics-guided detection modules under controlled conditions, we conducted synthetic injection-recovery experiments.

### 7.1 Cometary Dust Tail Injection-Recovery
We generated synthetic light curves spanning $N = 180\ \mathrm{days}$ at 29.4-minute cadence matching quiet Kepler targets ($\sigma_\mathrm{white} = 150\ \mathrm{ppm}$). We injected asymmetric cometary transits with varying peak depths ($\delta \in [500, 10000]\ \mathrm{ppm}$) and tail decay lengths ($\lambda_\mathrm{tail} \in [0.01, 0.10]$ phase units) into simulated stellar variability modeled as a Gaussian Process with a Matérn-$3/2$ kernel ($\rho = 5.0\ \mathrm{days}$, $\sigma_\mathrm{GP} = 200\ \mathrm{ppm}$).

The results demonstrate:
1. When light curves are pre-cleaned with a transit-masked detrending algorithm, cometary dust tails with depths $\delta \ge 1,500\ \mathrm{ppm}$ and asymmetry $\alpha \ge 0.30$ are recovered with a completeness of $94.2\%$ at a false positive rate $< 1.5\%$.
2. However, when grazing eclipsing binaries with out-of-transit ellipsoidal variation are injected without an explicit binary hypothesis test, the single-transit cometary fitter reports false-alarm detections ($\Delta\mathrm{BIC} > 100$) in $68\%$ of trials. This validates our finding that a secondary eclipse check is an absolute prerequisite before claiming a dust-tail detection.

### 7.2 Exomoon Perturbation Sensitivity Limits
We tested the multi-body photodynamic extractor by injecting 3-body planet-moon perturbations across a grid of satellite mass ratios $q = M_s / M_p \in [0.001, 0.10]$ for a Jupiter-mass planet ($P_p = 30\ \mathrm{d}$) with photometric timing noise $\sigma_\tau = 0.5\ \mathrm{minutes}$ across $N_\mathrm{epochs} = 16$ transits.

The recovery threshold ($\mathrm{SNR} \ge 3.0$ and $|\Delta\phi - 90^\circ| \le 15^\circ$) is achieved only for mass ratios $q \ge 0.012$ (an Earth-to-Jupiter mass ratio). For datasets with $N_\mathrm{epochs} \le 4$, the false-positive rate from white-noise fluctuations exceeds $40\%$, proving that exomoon claims cannot be justified without extended multi-year baselines.

## 8. Atmospheric Retrieval Benchmark (JWST WASP-39 b)

As an independent verification of the suite's physical modeling capabilities, the atmospheric module was evaluated on benchmark transmission spectrophotometry of the hot Saturn **WASP-39 b** obtained by the JWST Transiting Exoplanet Community ERS Team using NIRSpec PRISM ($0.5\text{--}5.5\ \mu\mathrm{m}$; Rustamkulov et al. 2023).

### Forward Model and Bayesian Inversion
The forward model computes transmission spectra using 1D plane-parallel radiative transfer under hydrostatic equilibrium:
$$R_p^2(\lambda) = R_{p,0}^2 + 2 \int_0^{z_\mathrm{max}} (R_{p,0} + z) \left( 1 - e^{-\tau(z, \lambda)} \right) dz$$
with cross-sections sampled from high-resolution line lists for $\mathrm{H_2O}$, $\mathrm{CO_2}$, $\mathrm{CH_4}$, and $\mathrm{CO}$.

Bayesian parameter estimation was executed via a conditional normalizing flow trained on $50,000$ synthetic atmospheres. The retrieved molecular abundances and $1\sigma$ credible intervals are:
- $\log(\mathrm{H_2O}) = -3.39^{+0.29}_{-0.30}$ (Literature: $\approx -3.3 \pm 0.4$)
- $\log(\mathrm{CO_2}) = -3.36^{+0.26}_{-0.27}$ (Literature: $\approx -3.4 \pm 0.3$)
- $\log(\mathrm{CH_4}) = -7.27^{+0.36}_{-0.36}$ (Depleted, consistent with chemical disequilibrium)
- Equilibrium Temperature $T_\mathrm{eq} = 1228 \pm 45\ \mathrm{K}$

The retrieved posterior medians match the published JWST reference values within $0.3\sigma$, validating the atmospheric inversion pipeline on genuine observational flight data.

## 9. Required Observational Protocols for Future Verification

To prevent spurious candidate detections from propagating into the literature, any future candidate surfaced by automated transit algorithms must satisfy the following minimum observational criteria before being announced:

1. **Phase Curve Completeness:** Phase-folded photometry must cover the full orbital cycle ($\phi \in [-0.5, 0.5]$). The presence of any dip near $\phi = 0.5$ with depth $> 3\sigma_\mathrm{phot}$ immediately disqualifies the candidate as a single planet with a dust tail unless the secondary eclipse is proven to be planetary thermal emission.
2. **Transit-Masked Detrending:** Baseline filtering must explicitly mask the transit window ($[-1.5 T_{14}, +1.5 T_{14}]$). Midpoint timing measurements must be verified against multiple independent filter window lengths ($W = 0.5, 1.0, 2.0\ \mathrm{days}$) and alternative algorithms (e.g., GP with a quasi-periodic kernel).
3. **Minimum Epoch Threshold:** No TTV-based exomoon candidate may be claimed with fewer than $N_\mathrm{transits} \ge 8$ high-SNR transit events.
4. **Rigorous Marginal Likelihood Integration:** Classifier heuristic scores must never be converted to posterior probabilities. Any Bayesian exomoon claim must publish the log-evidence difference:
   $$\Delta \ln \mathcal{Z} = \ln \mathcal{Z}_\mathrm{moon} - \ln \mathcal{Z}_\mathrm{planet} \ge 5.0$$
   computed via nested sampling over full 3-body photodynamical models.
5. **High-Contrast Imaging:** High-resolution adaptive optics (AO) imaging (e.g., with Keck/NIRC2 or Gemini/'Alopeke) is required to rule out background eclipsing binaries within the *Kepler* aperture ($4''$ pixel scale).

## 10. Discussion

The findings of this investigation provide an instructive case study at the intersection of machine learning, automated discovery pipelines, and observational astrophysics.

### The Siren Song of $\Delta\mathrm{BIC}$ in Big Data
In modern time-series surveys, datasets contain tens of thousands of data points. When standard information criteria such as BIC or AIC are computed under the assumption of independent Gaussian errors, the penalties for additional model parameters ($k \ln N$) scale logarithmically ($\sim 3 \ln 8431 \approx 27$), whereas unmodeled stellar variability or non-Gaussian systematic noise scales linearly with $N$. 

As a consequence, any model with sufficient morphological flexibility (such as an 8-parameter cometary extinction profile) can easily achieve $\Delta\chi^2 \sim 10^3\text{--}10^5$ over a rigid symmetric template simply by bending to follow correlated stellar noise or binary ellipsoidal variations. An automated pipeline that interprets $\Delta\mathrm{BIC} > 10$ as "strong evidence" without verifying model goodness-of-fit ($\chi^2_\nu \approx 1$) will inevitably yield astronomical false-positive rates.

### The Responsibility of "Reviewer #2" in AI-Assisted Astronomy
Artificial intelligence and automated optimization are indispensable tools for managing the data volumes of current and future surveys (such as the Vera C. Rubin Observatory and the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope). However, an AI pipeline does not possess physical common sense:
- It does not automatically check whether an 11 $R_\oplus$ object can physically be a sublimating rocky crust.
- It does not inherently know that three data points cannot constrain a 3-body satellite orbit.
- It will happily map an arbitrary internal metric to $99.9\%$ if its output transfer function is uncalibrated.

Computational astrophysics requires that every automated candidate be subjected to adversarial forensic auditing before publication.

## 11. Conclusions & Final Dispositions

We have conducted a thorough, conservative, peer-review-grade re-evaluation of four candidate exoplanetary signals from the Frontier Astronomy AI Discovery Suite:

1. **KIC 9944201:** The claimed disintegrating planet and dust tail are **refuted**. The system is an ultra-short-period ($P = 0.7215\ \mathrm{d}$) eclipsing binary exhibiting a $2.0\%$ primary eclipse, a $0.53\%$ secondary eclipse at phase $0.5$, and $4,151\ \mathrm{ppm}$ ellipsoidal variations. The claimed $\Delta\mathrm{BIC} \approx +73,043$ was a model-misspecification artifact.
2. **KIC 8494263:** The claimed exomoon is **refuted**. The analyzed baseline contains only three transits. The high TTV SNR ($50.1$) was an artifact of unmasked Savitzky-Golay detrending; true timing residuals are $\le 11\ \mathrm{seconds}$ ($\mathrm{SNR} \approx 0.19$). The $99.9\%$ posterior probability was an uncalibrated heuristic.
3. **KIC 10153011:** The claimed exomoon is **refuted**. With only three transits, the raw timing signal has an $\mathrm{SNR} = 2.20$, below the detection threshold.
4. **KIC 8308347:** The claimed Trojan planet is **refuted**. The target contains insufficient transit coverage, and the flagged dip is consistent with stellar spot modulation.

All four targets are definitively cataloged as **False Positives** or **Unverified Artifacts**. The detection algorithms and validation protocols have been updated to enforce mandatory secondary-eclipse checks, transit-masked baseline normalization, and strict nested-sampling Bayesian evidence integration.
